Learning to see depth
without being told.

Two slightly different signals enter a tiny neural network. Without any disparity labels during training, a shared representation learns to track the hidden shift between them.

03 / WHAT IS HAPPENING INSIDE

A very small network. A global constraint.

Every sampled position has the same 11–10–1 network. Learning updates a single shared set of 120 weights by comparing the output field at short and long spatial scales.

01

Local sensing

Five left-eye intensities and five right-eye intensities describe the local stereo neighbourhood. A constant bias completes 11 inputs.

x ∈ ℝ¹¹
02

Shared encoding

Ten tanh units transform the local patch into features. A linear readout projects these into one scalar response.

y = w₂ · tanh(W₁x)
03

Spatial learning

Convolutional averages of the outputs are compared at two scales. Gradients through the averages update the shared weights.

max F = log((V+ε)/(U+ε))

RESEARCH SCOPE IMPORTANT

A faithful 1D experiment, not a claim of full 2D replication.

This page demonstrates the repository's 1,000-position, one-dimensional stereo dataset and its spatial-invariance learning principle. The underlying Eglen–Bray–Stone paper also studies two-dimensional stereo fields, different stimuli and generalization to unseen patterns. Those results are not demonstrated by this dashboard.
